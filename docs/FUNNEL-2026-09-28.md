## 3. What the product asks after a result

**What a result page shows** [T `templates/job_detail.html:281-300`, `:347+`; `shared/scale_up.py`]:

- **Successful binder-design job** (bindcraft, pxdesign, rfantibody, rfdiffusion, proteina, iggm, mpnn, boltzgen, esmfold2-design): a card saying "Want more? Run N candidates on the same target — est. $X". N is 100, or 50 for boltzgen and 64 for esmfold2-design.
  - A top-up link appears only if the balance is short.
  - A "What next?" panel links to a ranomics.com blog explainer and to "Run this at scale", which opens the ranomics.com **contact form** (off-platform).
- **af2, colabfold, esmfold and boltz2:** no scale-up offer (`quote()` returns None). Only the "What next?" panel shows.
- **Failed or timed-out job:** neither panel. No retry, no "clone with fixes" and no explanation of what to change (both conditionals verified in the template; wording of the failure copy not audited).
- **Validate preset:** no scale-up.

**When a user first needs money** (inferred from [U] balance drops and [P]):

- Cheap tools cost cents per run: mpnn about 28 GPU-s, esmfold about 32 s, af2 about 191 s (median per successful run [U]).
- The regeneron.com user ran 21 of these on under $2.40.
- **A $20 credit is effectively unlimited for folding and sequence tools.**
- Binder trials are where money starts to matter:
  - bindcraft trial: "About $6.30" [P `/tools/bindcraft`]
  - one bindcraft run: about $8 observed [U]
  - one boltzgen run: about $7.20 observed [U]
- So $20 buys roughly **two to three binder trials**, and the first real need for money is the 100-candidate scale-up.
- **Zero** `credit_exhausted` events exist across all accounts [U]. Nobody has ever hit $0.

**What pricing and wallet copy say** [P `/pricing`]:

- "New accounts start with a $20 wallet balance, usable for 30 days … enough to try any tool on your own inputs before you top up".
- $20 minimum top-up, non-refundable.
- "Unused balance does not expire". Read next to the 30-day signup credit this is ambiguous: it means topped-up money, but it sits close to the credit sentence.
- **No per-run example prices.** The tool pages show a live estimate, but only in the form.

## 5. Ranked causes

### 1. Nothing on the platform ever makes a user need to pay

**Confidence: high.**

Evidence:

- Zero accounts have hit $0 [U].
- 15 of 16 one-run leavers still hold $4.90 to $19.90 [U].
- 24 of 27 multi-run users never topped up, including users with 14 and 21 runs [U].
- Folding and sequence tools cost cents (inferred from GPU-s [U]).
- The only in-app paid prompt is the binder scale-up card. It shows a top-up link only when the balance is short, and never shows on folding tools [T].
- Leaving is costless. Paying only becomes relevant at the 100-candidate step, which most users never reach.

Fix or experiment (**S**):

- Put the scale-up card, with price, on every successful result, including af2, colabfold and esmfold (a batch of N or the next-step tool).
- Show the price even when the balance covers it.
- Measure with the existing `scale_up_click` event, which has **zero** occurrences to date [U].

Structural change (**M**, Leo's call):

- Smaller signup credit, or credit scoped to binder trials, or a per-account free-run cap on cheap tools. The credit is currently $20 [U] and was $5 before 08-18.
- The past $5 era did not produce more top-ups (1 of about 55; inferred from month rows), so shrinking the credit alone is unlikely to help.

### 2. Binder-tool first runs fail and the failed page is a dead end

**Confidence: high on the fact, medium on how much it costs.**

Evidence:

- bindcraft first runs failed 4 of 5; esmfold2-design 2 of 2 [U].
- The buyers most likely to pay are binder users, and all 5 failed-first-run leavers were binder users [U].
- Two of them opened the top-up page and left [U].
- A failed job shows no scale-up and no "what next" panel, so there is no retry path [T].
- #364 targets first-run failure, but there are zero runs since it shipped [H][U].

Fix or experiment:

- (**S**) On a failed or timed-out job, add "what went wrong and what to change" plus a one-click clone of the job with its settings. State the refund if one applied.
- (**M**) Re-check bindcraft and esmfold2-design failure causes once post-#364 runs exist.

### 3. The next step after a result sends people off-platform

**Confidence: medium.**

Evidence:

- The result page is the last page for 16 of 16 one-run leavers, and 6 of them viewed nothing after it [U].
- The "Run this at scale" link goes to a ranomics.com contact form [T]. That is a sales lead, not a self-serve purchase, and there is no admin evidence of how many clicked (downloads and outbound clicks are not tracked [T `static/js/track.js`]).

Fix or experiment (**S**):

- Track the outbound click and downloads.
- A/B test making the in-app scale-up the primary button, with contact as secondary.

### 4. Binder forms lose half to three-quarters of openers before submit

**Confidence: medium.**

Evidence:

- Form-to-run is 25 to 50% for binder tools against 55 to 65% for folding tools [U].
- Binder forms need a structure, a chain and hotspot residues [P].
- 37 users opened a form and never ran, and binder tools lead that list (boltzgen 6, bindcraft 5, rfantibody 4) [U].

Fix or experiment:

- (**S**) Put a "Load example" button (the example run already exists) above the fold on every binder form.
- (**M**) Pass Scout results straight into the form. The bindcraft page says Scout "hands the target and the residues back into this form" [P]; how often that handoff is used was not measured.

### 5. Most accounts never come back after day one

**Confidence: medium on the size, low on the cause.**

Evidence:

- Only 39 of 134 came back on a later day [U]. August was the low point, at 2 of 40.
- The only automatic pull-back is the re-engagement email, and its cron is unverified [T].
- 22 users came only for free tools and were never shown a GPU tool [U].

Fix or experiment:

- (**S**) Confirm on Railway that `cron/reengagement.py` and `cron/signup_credit.py` run, and log each send somewhere admin can see it.
- (**M**) On Scout results, add a "design a binder against this epitope" button that opens a prefilled GPU form.

### 6. Signup gate turned away real prospects (before #363)

**Confidence: medium that it happened, none on whether #363 fixed it.**

Evidence:

- A handful from pharma and university domains never got in [R].

## 7. What if the signup credit had only ever been $5?

Question (Leo, via hub lead, 2026-09-28): how many of the 134 customer accounts would have had to top up if every account had only $5 of credit?

**Method** [U, re-scraped 2026-09-28 from all 145 `/admin/users/<id>` pages; the 11 internal accounts excluded as in section 1]:

- **Net spend** is the sum of every wallet row of type `hold`, `hold_release`, `charge` and `absorbed_variance`, sign flipped. This is run debits minus the surplus returned.
- Excluded rows:
  - `signup_credit` and `topup`, as instructed.
  - `adjustment` rows. None of the 134 customer accounts has one.
  - Legacy `ledger` rows. No customer account has a ledger spend row.
- Failed runs cost $0 where the hold was fully released. Some failed or timed-out runs were charged for GPU time used.
- **Per-run cost:**
  - Each `hold` is paired with the run logged within about 1 second of it, and with its `hold_release` in first-in, first-out order.
  - `charge` and `absorbed_variance` rows go to the nearest preceding run.
  - Per-run costs summed back to the account's net spend for all 134 accounts, to within $0.02.
- **The run that crossed $X** is the first run, in time order, at which cumulative settled cost exceeds $X.
- Net spend equals each account's balance drop. It was spot-checked against the wallet balances in section 2 for med.uni-duesseldorf.de, the two Caltech-lab gmail accounts and foxmail.com.

- **9 of the 10 crossed $5 on a binder-design run** (rfantibody, bindcraft, boltzgen or rfdiffusion). The tenth crossed $5 on a charged af2 timeout, after 8 cheaper runs [U].
- **4 accounts would have hit $0 on their very first binder run**: med.uni-duesseldorf.de, the two Caltech-lab accounts, and dartmouth.edu's first paid run. Their first run alone costs $7 to $8. With $5 of credit the estimate would have been refused at submit, so they would have met a top-up prompt *before* seeing any result. This is inferred from hold sizes of $16 for bindcraft and similar amounts for boltzgen [U]; the submit-time refusal behaviour is inferred and was not re-checked in code.
- **Only 2 of the 10 actually topped up.** kangwon.ac.kr did so while still on $5 credit, and topped up on 05-29, *before* its net spend crossed $5 on 06-04 [U]. cam.ac.uk topped up the day it ran past its real $15 credit [U].
- The other 8 are exactly the binder users whose credit was raised to $15 or $20. Under a $5 credit, each would have met a paywall mid-work.
- **124 of 134 never spent over $5.** For them the credit level made no difference to whether they needed to pay [U].

### Caveat

The 108 accounts that received $15 or $20 had more to spend, and may have run more or larger jobs *because* they had it. A $5 account facing a top-up prompt might have run fewer or smaller jobs, or left, or paid. These figures only count who spent past each threshold under the credit they actually had. They do not predict what anyone would have done with less, and no attempt is made to model that.

Side note [U]: 10 customer accounts still hold a `hold` with no matching `hold_release`, including kangwon.ac.kr, cam.ac.uk and med.uni-duesseldorf.de. The amounts reconcile with their balances, so these look like settled-by-charge or older-model rows rather than stuck money (inferred, not verified in code).
