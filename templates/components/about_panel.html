What good looks like

Each tool reports a subset of these depending on whether it does design, sequence recovery, or structure prediction.

ipTM
{# Same correction as templates/help/tool_guide.html, and this is the copy's THIRD home — an independent review found two of the three. "The binder to target interface" is the metric's intent, not what every tool stores: on a multi-chain target rfdiffusion, pxdesign and bindcraft report a number computed over the whole complex, and so did boltzgen before the August 2026 container update. See shared/metric_glossary.py. #} {# The cut-off is READ from shared/metric_glossary.py, not typed here. It used to say "aim above roughly 0.7" while the runtime card ~40 lines further down the SAME page carried a tool's own output_summary saying ipTM >= 0.65 — a page contradicting itself within one scroll. 0.65 is the value with a source behind it: shared/score_legends.py sets rfdiffusion's ipTM "good" at 0.65 and the glossary's acceptable band agrees. 0.7 was in this template and nowhere else. ``metric_glossary`` is a jinja global (app.py) holding shared.metric_glossary.GLOSSARY, so it is visible here even though every form imports this macro WITHOUT context. #}
Predicted confidence in the contact between two chains, on a 0 to 1 scale. Higher is better: {{ metric_glossary.get('ipTM', {}).get('good_range', '') }}. Individual tools set their own pass bar a little either side of that — the guide for the tool you ran states its one. On a multi-chain target the number may cover the target's own chain–chain interface as well as the binder's.
pLDDT
Per-residue confidence in the predicted fold. Higher means the model is more sure of that part of the structure.
i_pAE and pAE
Predicted alignment error, at the interface (i_pAE) or across the whole structure (pAE). Lower is better.
ProteinMPNN recovery
